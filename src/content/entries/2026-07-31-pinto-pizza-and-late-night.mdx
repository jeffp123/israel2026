## Venturing and the Discovery of Pizza Pinto

Nuriya was still so tired and just wanted to sit down. She slept for 18 hours, had drank water, and even had an apple. But, I think she was still lacking a meal of protein and other food groups.

I brought her to Ben Yehuda where almost every possible kosher food is available. She wanted pizza. That would be easy.

We went to the first pizza place that we saw, Pinto, and it was surprisingly empty.  Nuriya decided this was the place, so we ordered a pizza. They would only make a whole pizza (not just slices) and I didn't have lactaid, so they made one side half crust/sauce (no cheese).`

[Some pictures of Nuriya at the elevator of the building, and next to some of the many cats we stopped for along the way, and just one picture at the pizza place that has such a memorable story.]

<Photos glob="/src/assets/2026-07-30/203355-pizza-dinner/*.jpg" />

While we were sitting there, a father and son came in. I noticed they made a special point to kiss the mezuza which was a little bit away from the entrance.

Nuriya sat and ate pizza and did her Duolingo and I could tell she was regaining energy.

At some point the guy who made the pizza (the owner?) came out and told me that when I showed up with Nuriya we had reminded him of the father/son who were there (who he knew of because they are regulars there). When he saw us, he thought of them, and moments later they arrived. He said something about how thinking about someone can be powerful.

I got to talking to them and the father spoke fluent English, having come from Houston 11 years ago. He invited us to come with him and his son to a video game store across the street.

[Reflecting, this was one of the most special moments. I was getting worried that Nuriya had slept so much and wasn't eating, and so in this moment I was just so grateful that she found a pizza place that she liked and was actually eating and being energetic for maybe the first time since we arrived. But, this moment was so much more. How the owner came out to us and tell that little story about how just thinking about a person can bring them was such an only-in-Jerusalem moment.

And we really connected with the father and son. I got to talking to him because I went over to get a drink out of the refridgerator next to where they were sitting and the son was in the way. They were learning/reading something together and I apologized for interupting and there was an immediate connection. We talked and it turns out he and his son had weekly tradition to get pizza at Pinto and then go to G@me Zone across the street, which they invited us to join them.

When we all left Pinto together the owner ran out to us and said "You still owe me..." I panicked, wondering if I forgot to pay. The owner continued "... for the friendship".

Such an only-in-Jerusalem moment. I am reflecting on how my main focus was initially worry about if Nuriya will eat, and then to have such an warm and nice experience. There were many such experiences like this throughout the trip.

Nuriya would go on to say that Pinto is some of the best pizza she has ever had. (I agree!) And so we made a point to try to repeat that experience, throughout the rest of the trip. For the next ~26 days, we went to Pinto every few nights. It was not open any other time. We do know how we kept missing it, but all the more special that it was open when we walked by it on this night.]
