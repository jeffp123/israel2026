One of my favorite, unexpected moments in Israel includes acquiring food for my children. Early one morning, my daughter went to the shuk with her father and got a freshly squeezed pomegranate juice. It’s possibly her favorite fruit, but alas she got a stomach ache and came back to the apartment in a state of discomfort. We had planned to go to the chocolate farm that day and I was determined to help her feel better. One of the things Israel is known for is fresh pita. So I asked her if she’d like some and she did. I went to the mini market, walking through the hot stony streets, winding their way to Ein Gedi Rd and landed in a small shop that has a sample of everything. Alas, the bread section was very disappointing, with a bag of moldy pita sitting there. I almost left disappointed for the first time, in the land of abundance, but got enough nerve to approach the shop owner and asked: “ יש פיתה חדשה?

Do you have fresh (new) pita?

The man smiled and pointed for me to go out. I understand enough Hebrew to know he wasn’t telling me to leave but couldn’t quite find what he was talking about, so he told another worker there to show me. As I exited, there was an oven, he told me to open it and I did, there was fresh warm pita ready for the taking. I was so happy! I took 5 and he put it in a bag for me, then I paid and went home. My daughter enjoyed it and we went to the chocolate farm. The stomach ache was gone and so was my disappointment.

I still feel the joy of fresh pita in my mind.
