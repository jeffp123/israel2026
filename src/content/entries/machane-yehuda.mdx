## Arrival
Am sitting in apartment at Mesilat Yesharim 17. Nuriya is up reading Story Thieves 2 on the Kindle that I brought.

I am having Paskez Slim Thins crackers (like they sell at The Crown) with Gush Halav cheese that the host of this AirBnB left for us.

We had some minor electrical drama before.  At around 10pm IST, when Yair and I were both up from out respective naps after landing, I told him it was OK to plug in his retainer cleaner and that all normal America products will work fine with 240v Israeli outlets and well, I was wrong. It sparked and shorted, causing me to ask the host where the breaker is. We got the power back on and I now I would know where the breaker was.

This was apparently useful information because later when I got back from a street roam and shopping for some things, Nuriya got up after 12am IST (after a 10 hour nap).  And she told me the bathroom lights would not turn on.  In fact many, but not all, things would not turn on.

I looked at the breaker and everything was fine. I then davened maariv and then came to check it again, more closely this time. As it turned out the breaker box included a Shabbat timer!  I just needed to switch the timer to “on”.

I love how it was a simple solution, but also one that I would have been completely helpless to solve if not from the previous shorting of the circuit due to the retainer cleaner. A nice hasgacha pratis moment.

We arrived in Israel almost 15 hours ago. The plane ride was a nice as is expected all sitting together for 11 hours, but BH uneventful and we could appreciate the wonder of flying to Israel. We did really well navigating from the airport, to the baggage checkout, to the train to Jerusalem, to the train along Jaffa Street to Dadvika, and to our apartment at Messilat Yesharim 17. Nuriya insisted on only taking elevators and stairs and not escalators, but even with that, we did great!

The location is so nice, right next to so many familiar streets, a very quick walk to Machane Yehuda.

When we got here at around 1 or 2pm IST, I unpacked a little as Yair and Nuriya went to take naps. I then did the same,
setting an alarm for 2 hours later. I remember turning off the alarm but somehow slept for 6 hours.

I decided to go shopping because I really wanted to get to walk the streets of Jerusalem!  I checked on the kids and kinda woke Yair just to let him know what time it was in case he wanted to get up. We talked, I told him that I was going to shop, learned about what happens when you plug a 120v retainer cleaner into a 240v outlet, and after this I felt more confident about leaving to go on a walk. (I was considering leaving a note if Yair hadn’t gotten up, but I would have been worried the whole time.)

So, the walk… was absolutely wonderful!  The streets of Jerusalem were alive with people doing so many things.  I cannot describe the feeling of being here in our ancient homeland and to be around such life, so much diversity of Jewish cultures tangling together as I walked out of a little alley and across Jaffa street and somehow ended up on a very busy pedestrian area that I assume is part of the Machane Yehuda shuk. There were bars and restaurants and different music playing at each intersection. The name of the street was Eitz Hayim..

Jaffa Street, and landed on a busy pedestrian stretch, a different song at every
intersection. The sign read Eitz Hayim.
