[This entry is almost entirely my recollections of the day, being written a couple months later.]

## Everyone Got Up Late

The heatwave was seemingly still in effect, as far as I remember anyway.

We all got up a little late. As noted at the end of the previous entry, today's plans involved:
* Go to the Biblical Museum of Natural History in Beit Shemesh, where Nuriya can pet the chinchillas, like she remembered from two years prior.
* Get lunch at IKEA (because how cool is it that there is an IKEA with kosher dining!)
* Head back to pick up Moshe from Jerusalem Hatzalah Headquarters at 78 Yirmiyahu Street, at around 8pm, before the others head to the airport.

I had been in touch with a specialist regarding a concern that I had about a developing recurrent hernia, and I had a thought that I would see if I could fit in a quick trip to his office in Tel Aviv during our travels. However, with the idea that I would be picking up Moshe in Jerusalem at 8pm, I realized it might not work.

However, Moshe then decided that he wants to go with everybody to the airport, schlepping his bags and all, just so he could finish the trip with everybody at the airport (or some reason like that). And so, I thought now this will all work perfectly! Because Tel Aviv is basically right next to the airport, right? (I clearly did not understand distance or travel time.)

And so, the new plan was to go from the IKEA to Tel Aviv (somehow) and then to the airport to pick up Moshe. And it was a solid plan.

Except, we all woke up late!

And that set the pace for things not exactly working as planned, and yet everything was ok.

# To the Bus

This would be our first adventure where we leave Jerusalem, and we were still trying to get used to travel and efficiently traveling together with our different paces, Yair, Nuriya, and I.

We took a local bus to the Central Bus Station. I have memories of the Jerusalem Central Bus Station going back almost more than 20 years. I was trying to figure out if we needed to buy a ticket, like was the case 20 years ago, or if we could use the Rav Kav and just add the ride while on the bus. I didn't know where to go, but we went to the third floor and eventually found where our bus was to depart from. The number was there and it was the time when the bus was supposed to be leaving. We got there just in time. Except, nobody was there. I looked closer and there was a sign that said that the bus was leaving from another location _behind the train station_. What does that even mean?

<Photos glob="/src/assets/2026-08-04/130355-beit-shemesh-bus-adventure/*.jpg" />

I went to ask for for help at an information booth and somebody seemed annoyed when he repeated to me that the bus is coming to the stop "behind the train station". Yes, I got that, but where is that? When he decided that he was done talking to me, he closed the information booth window.

We all left the bus station in search of "behind the train station". As it turns out this was a bus stop that is indeed behind the train station, at a very populated set of multiple bus stops, looking out towards the Chord Bridge. We found the correct stop, but the bus did not seem to be coming. Instead, a bus with a similar number, also going to Beit Shemesh stopped. I asked the driver if it is the right bus and did not understand his response.

This happened a couple more times with busses that seemed to be going to Beit Shemesh, but not the same number. Some people tried to help us. Someone told me to install Moovit, so I did, but was still confused.

Yair then suggested something really nice. He said (something to the effect of) "Let's just get on a bus going towards Beit Shemesh so we are not just waiting here." Great idea! We were all feeling a little impatient and/or adventerous anyway. Plus, I was worried that we would miss our time slot at the museum, as I already had to call them to move it up once. So, we got on the next bus to beit shemesh!

## Beit Shemesh Bus Adventure

On the way to Beit Shemesh, I talked to someobdoy on the bus who spoke fluent English. We both noticed that our GPS signal on our phones was way off, which is something that I undersatnd that the IDF does to scramble the signal to prevent potential attacks. What I did not realize is that it sometimes just happens with no explanation.

I told him where we are going and he explained that this bus is going to right past the museum, but it doesn't stop there. It keeps going, further into Beit Shemesh. Whoops. Well, it is an adventure.

We kept going and got off at the first stop in Beit Shemesh where there would be another bus to connect back and head to the museum. It was very very hot out, and while it was so nice to just be there in Beit Shemesh, and I wanted to walk around a little while we wait for the bus, Yair and Nuriya wanted to sit in the shade of the bus stop.

We got on the next bus, taking a small tour of Beit Shemesh, which led to another bus station / parking lot that was right across the street from the museum. Except the street was a major highway and it would have been a 15 minute walk if we followed all of the proper paths to go across. And so, with about 10 minutes until our designated time on our ticket to the museum left, I finally used Gett to call a taxi - something that the kids wanted me to do way before all of this! We had a nice 5 minute ride to the museum, the whole time the driver on a call with the next person who he was picking up, and then we were at the museum!

## Going to Tel Aviv

I had been in touch with the hernia specialist who I was thinking of squeezing in a visit before picking up Moshe. Everything was later than expected so I didn't think it would work out. Also, at IKEA, just checking Google Maps, I was starting to realize that it would take a while to get to Tel Aviv and then to the airport to pick up Moshe.

I send a message to say that I won't make it that day. I think any American doctor or specialist would probably just offer to reschedule at that point. This doctor said that he has a friend who has a taxi service and could get us there straight from the IKEA, and he gave me his number. And so, in this noisy IKEA, I left the table and I called him. He only spoke Hebrew, which made things very interesting. And yet, somehow we communicated with eachother and with getting this doctor on the other line, he managed to come pick us up and take us to Tel Aviv. The whole thing was amid so many moving parts that could potentially go wrong, and yet it was an easy and smooth experience getting there.

And so we headed to Tel Aviv as the sun was setting.

<Photos glob="/src/assets/2026-08-04/191851-driving-to-tel-aviv/*.jpg" />

## Tel Aviv

I do not have many pictures from Tel Aviv. But, I did get to the appointment. I will leave the details out of this public post, but I will say that I appreciated the time and attention from this hernia specialist while Yair and Nuriya hung out in the little waiting room. And even though I ultimately did not end up getting an evaluation from him, the whole experience was just so interesting and not like anything I have experienced in America with a visit to a doctor. And while I did not exactly get the answers that I wanted, the whole visit and just talking to him, gave me some comfort about what I was previously worried about. Looking back at this experience from the vantage point of a couple months later, I understand that this was exactly what I needed, nothing more and nothing less.

And after that, we were off to pick up Moshe.

<Photos glob="/src/assets/2026-08-04/211707-tel-aviv-arrival/*.jpg" />

In retrospect, maybe we should have taken a taxi at this point, but Yair and Nuriya were willing to do more bus travel and I wanted to get to walk around Tel Aviv and not just be transported one place to another. And so, our next adventure involved a series of switching busses and trains and lots of waiting and wondering when our bus or train will arrive and if we are at the correct location, etc. It was all a real adventure.

I remember that once we got on the first bus, after waiting a long time for it and not sure which stop, Yair asked me for a wireless charger since his phone battery was low. When looking for the charger in my bag, I noticed that I didn't have my phone! I panicked. Like really panicked, how are we going to get in contact with Moshe? And with Yair's battery low, and already running late, and not knowing how to switch busses. I searched through my bag. I tried to retrace where I last remember having it. Yair couldn't even call my phone becuase his battery was low. After a few minutes, I realized my phone was in one of my pockets (or something like that) the whole time! I say this story to illustrate that things were a little tense, mostly with the timing and lack of clarity about where we are going. And yet, everything worked perfectly B"H!

We got to the airport. We looked for Moshe. We called, we texted, we used the Find My app. He was there. We were there. We couldn't see eachother and yet it was clear that we were in the same general area. And then, we found eachother!

And Nuriya, who had not seen Moshe for over five weeks, ran and hugged and would not let go for some time.
