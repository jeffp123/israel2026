## Early morning

Did not sleep much last night. Tried melatonin, but maybe not enough (1mg) or maybe just too excited. Was up at around 7am to the sounds of Yair and Nuriya talking about breakfast or something. Yair was well rested. Nuriya, not as much.

Yair was also determined this morning to be able to plug in his laptop so he could participate in the voting for the "countdown"-style game that he made for the game jam.  He needed a three-prong plug adapter. We exited the apartment, for Yair and Nuriya the first time since arriving in Israel about 20 hours before, and I showed them the way to Agripas where we almost immediately found a store that sells hardware, tools, and plug adapters. After this easy accomplishment, we headed back. A quick outing.

I then helped Nuriya unpack more and asked if she wants to go out and explore more, and if so to just say the word. She wanted to read, which was good because I was now getting very tired. But, within a few minutes she came in and said "the word". So, we went.

<Photos glob="/src/assets/2026-07-29/092813-nachlaot-morning-walk/*.jpg" />

I could tell Nuriya was very tired too, but we walked around a little farther, stopping at a bakery and various stores.

<Photos glob="/src/assets/2026-07-29/101949-stationery-shop/*.jpg" />

We walked into a really interesting and strange "mall" that had a few open stores, but mostly lots of stairs below a skylight. It reminded me of The Backrooms.

<Photos glob="/src/assets/2026-07-29/103133-jerusalem-mall-atrium/*.jpg" />

I want to comment at this point (as I update this entry after the trip), that I would later find out that this that this mall is called Binyon Klal and it is such an interesting place. The whole mall has a feel like it is old and abandoned. The escalators do not work and there are broken windows and various empty places where there used to be stores. And yet, there are also various businesses that are open and people coming and going.

Thinking back, it is ironic that this is where we journeyed to on our first walk outside of the apartment. Here, I wanted to show Nuriya the beauty of Jerusalem and indeed this mall is surrounded by beatiful streets and buildings all around, next to Davidka Square, and yet we ended up going to such a dreary run down place. And yet we must have felt compelled to enter by the fact that it is such also such an interesting place, as a preservation of older archictecture style and representing a piece of modern Israeli history. Apparently it was the site of what was formerly a Jewish trade school from 1882, although was reconstructured in the 1970s, with various memorials to the former school found in it.

We also went to a grocery store in the same building, where I had a memory of three years ago when I went there with Moshe and Eshka and got Kinder Eggs. I'm not sure if it was the same store, but again with the broken windows and just not in great shape. And yet people shopping and going about life. And in the middle of a such an otherwise nice, well-taken-care-of area. It just seemed like such a strange anomaly. Both that these structures are as they are, and that this is where Nuriya and I ended up venturing, both of us feeling very tired.

I had wanted for Nuriya to eat something and we passed by so many amazing restaurants, but Nuriya wanted Bisli from the grocery store, so that is what we got.

We briefly walked into the Mahane Yehuda shuk before heading back.

<Photos glob="/src/assets/2026-07-29/105204-mahane-yehuda/*.jpg" />

# Back at the apartment

We headed back and I went to rest while Nuriya watched Loud House and Yair participated in the game jam voting.

The plan was that I would take a 1.5 hour nap and then we would go to the kotel, passing through the Malcha Mall where I would get a new apple watch to replace the one that I forgot to bring on this trip. After that, the plan was that Ben Skoler would come by, we would have pizza and play a game before leaving for Yair's planned meeting with a local gaming group at 7:30pm.

(Note, while updating this after the trip: This was our second day in Jerusalem and I had mixed up the Malha Mall with the Mamiilla Mall, so this would not have worked anyway, but just for context: I forgot my Apple Watch and I was very much wanting to track steps and other things, and at this point in the trip, was very adamant about picking one up a new one while in Israel. This feeling would eventually fade into a realization that I don't need such things. But anyway, continuing with the story...)

What actually happened was that I slept for over four hours. Nuriya lost track of time and Yair got very into the game voting and was insistent on watching a several hour long streamer who was supposedly going to play his game on his live stream, just as long as Yair didn't leave the stream. I got up before 5, so now the new plan was to just get to the kotel before going to a board gaming group that Yair had found and wanted to attend. But, first I needed to play Yair's game jam game, which I had not yet done.

We headed out, Nuriya still tired, and now hungry. We walked along Jaffa Street, stopping for Nuriya to play piano in the street, and then to the Rova, got pizza, and then to the kotel!

(Edit: I would later find out what this was, which would turn out to be one of the most amazing experiences that I would have in this trip, but we were not standing at the correct location so it just appeared like there were some random blinking laser lights in the sky, and honestly even watching the videos below, I don't remember noticing much. ## At the Talpiot Mall with Nuriya

We passed various options for ice cream (Katzefet), which Nuriya did not want, explaining that it is night time and not to have sugar now! But we did go straight to the Hadar Mall.

We went to a book store and checked out some games there, went to rebar (just for me, Nuriya did not want), and back to pick up Yair on a bus, followed by a crowded bus ride back.

<Photos glob="/src/assets/2026-07-29/210212-mall-shopping/*.jpg" />

Recalling some events after the trip as I put together this entry:

At the book/game store in the mall, I pointed at a Hebrew Pokemon-themed Monopoly (as far I recall, writing this part from memory after the trip) and remarked at how interesting it is that there is an American game with a Japenese theme in Hebrew, how that blends so many cultures. One of the people in the store, who spoke English, overheard and pointed out that Super Mario Brothers is even more diverse at incorporating cultural influences. I don't remember exactly what he said (something about it being a Japanese game about plumbers from Brooklyn and some other themes about the magic mushrooms?), but it was a nice moment and seemed to match the theme of Yair being at a gaming group, with Nuriya and I at the nearby mall geeking out with this person in the book/game store about various related topics.

Just before leaving the mall, I took some time to set up my Rav Kav so we could take a bus back to where Yair was. This would be our first bus ride in Jerusalem on this trip (not counting the couple stops along the light rail when first getting to Jerusalem), so it felt like a big deal.

We met up with Yair outside the building that he was gaming in on Ben Dor. We then proceeded to wait for the 78 bus (a bus that we would later be very aquainted with and which Nuriya was already commenting about how the modern State of Israel is 78 years old, just like the bus is number 78!). While waiting for the bus, I remember we were all very tired (still with jet lag). I said to Yair to take a look at the amazing view from the Tayelet while we are waiting for the bus. He declined, saying "I don't want to do tourist stuff". I am writing this, reflecting back on how we would all be in this same spot just a couple of weeks later participating in the nightly festival along the Tayelet, and how Yair would attend many more game nights in this area, and how we would have later moments appreciating the absolutely amazing view. Indeed, there was no need to rush to check something out while exhausted. We were not in Israel to be "tourists". We could take one moment at a time and appreciate everything in the right time.

## Late night on Jaffa Road

After the children were settled (and letting Yair know!) I went back out in search of falafel and hummus. I walked around the shuk, saw the Breslov guy with the guitar playing Carlebach niggunim again, and so much lively action all around the streets even after 12am. I thought it would be easy to find a falafel place, with so many restaurants open. Surprisingly, I could not! Every place had shwarma, burgers, chicken, and even goose! But, no falafel! It was fun trying to search, although I was so tired and decided to get back and write this (which I am finishing the next day because I was too tired last night).
