Sometime people say, I have no words.

Well the opposite can also be true.

After my recent trip to Israel with all the kids, husband and parents in-law (in their 80’s) there is so much to reflect on. Everyone was pretty excited and talking in turns didn’t seem to work, hence the title.

Without describing some part of the experience, the whole will get lost, but no matter how much I write, it will still not be enough.

Let’s just say, there is still room for new friendly Israelis to enter the picture. So I made a new friend who is a 3 generation store owner in the heart of Jerusalem and after he made Israel logo t- shirts for my kids, and helped cheer up my teenager daughter by showing her the Ben Yehuda skirt every seminary girl needs to have, I sent him my poetry via WhatsApp upon request.

The night before flying back very early morning, my husband and I made it to the Kotel. It’s hard to express how amazing it is to see a full plaza of people of all ages at 12:30 am saying slichot to HaShem. It’s enough to say we did not want to leave.

And this is where calling the Jewish People - the people of the book- becomes inaccurate.

Maybe at some point in Jewish History we were called that due to some studious Rabbis. But now, we are so much more than that. People don’t pour into the streets to study, but people do to speak to God. This didn’t feel like a custom or an obligation but a true experience of unity and joy in palpable prayer.

No matter how focused and trained the chazzan is in the American synogogue, nothing can compare to the raw and moving singing of what we became a part of at the Kotel last Sunday night.
